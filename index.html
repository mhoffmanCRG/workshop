Setting up the job

  1. Turn the stock to the outside diameter shown on the “Turn stock to” tile and hold it in the rotary axis. Support long stock with a tailstock.
  2. Set the cross-axis zero on the rotary centreline. An error here makes every groove lopsided, so indicate it carefully.
  3. Set Z zero to match the “Z zero” setting, with the tip of the ball-nose cutter as the reference.
  4. Set “Rotary head is on the” to the side of the table your rotary axis sits on, then set the long-axis zero so the “start face position” is the face the cutter enters from, normally the free end.
  5. Measure how far the cutter sticks out below the holder and enter it as “Length below holder”.
  6. If the top edges are tilted, jog the rotary positive, watch which way the top of the stock moves, and set “Rotary + turns stock top toward” to match.
  7. Run the program in air first, with Z zero raised, and watch the rotary through one full groove and one full turn.

With tilt off and cross-axis offset positioning, the rotary only moves between grooves, so it can be locked while cutting and backlash does not affect the tooth form. With tilt on, it also turns between the top-edge passes.

Pulley
Groove profile

Nominal HTD 5M values, set when the belt is chosen in the title along with a cutter size and step-down that suit the groove. Check them against your belt or the supplier's drawing. A groove radius equal to the cutter radius cuts the groove bottom in one pass with no side clearance.

Cutter

With feeds set from the material, the cutting feed is a typical chip load per tooth for that material and cutter size, times the flutes and the spindle speed; untick it to enter your own. The cutting feed is for a full-depth cut on the side of the cutter. With adjustment on, a pass that only skims the metal is fed faster to keep the same chip thickness per tooth, and a pass that cuts low on the ball, where the cutter turns slowly, is fed slower. Tilting the cutter moves the cut up the ball, so tilted passes run faster.

Cutting

Centre slotting plunges an upright cutter down the middle of the groove, so the slow-moving tip does the roughing. Side cutting starts at one side with the part turned as far as it will go, then works down from alternate sides with the cutter leaning, so the shaft and the side of the ball do the cutting. It takes more passes. Near the groove bottom the cutter fits the groove too closely to lean much, so the lower part of the ball still cuts there. Tick “finishing passes only” when the grooves are already roughed out, for example to re-run the finish; the passes then come down the groove from both sides in turn.

Pass by pass makes each pass on every groove before the next one. The cutter keeps its lean for the whole pass, so the rotary only steps from groove to groove and never winds back. It turns the least with side cutting.

Machine setup

With tilt on, each tooth-tip pass turns the part so the widest part of the ball does the cutting, not its slow-moving tip. The cutter works alongside the stock, so the part is never turned further than the cutter's length below the holder allows, keeping the holder 1 mm above the stock. “Finish on the shaft” then pushes the tilted cutter in along its own axis so its straight side does the finishing; 0 keeps the cut on the ball. To set the direction, jog the rotary positive and watch which way the top of the stock moves (cross axis is Y when the rotary lies along X). If it is wrong the cutter will be on the wrong side of the tooth.

Cross-axis offset keeps the rotary still while a groove is cut. Rotary rotation keeps the cross axis at 0 and turns the part to reach the flanks.

Choosing the head’s side also sets “Stock runs toward” so the start face is the free end of the stock; change it back if you zero on the face next to the head. The drawings look at the free end, and the 3D view shows the head as close as the cutter and holder allow.

Lead-in and lead-out are how far the cutter centre travels past each face. Keep both at least the cutter radius so plunges happen in air. Shorten lead-out only if the groove must stop at a shoulder.
